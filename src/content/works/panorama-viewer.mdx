<Prose title="Abstract">

In the past, we could only experience places we had never visited through
pictures or video, where the viewing angle is fixed to whatever the camera
was pointed at and cannot be explored freely. As panoramic images became
common, we gained the ability to roam a scene from any viewpoint on a
computer or mobile device, rotating freely to look around — much like Google
Maps Street View.

Beyond Street View, anyone can shoot panoramic images with a panorama camera
and assemble their own virtual tour through a panorama viewer. In existing
viewers, users must click a scene-switch button on screen to move between
scenes, which interrupts the immersive feeling of the tour.

We propose a new panorama viewer and a set of panorama editing tools. A
finger-zoom gesture triggers the scene transition, and a scene-set
architecture reduces the frame clutter of large virtual tours. Editors build
a tour through the editing tools, and visitors move between scenes with an
intuitive zoom gesture while they browse.
